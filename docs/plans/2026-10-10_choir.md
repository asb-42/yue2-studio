# Choir alignment ("conductor") — plan

Status: **closed 2026-10-10** — Phase 2 gate failed, idea dropped per §6. Phases 3–5 do not proceed. Kept: `align.rs`, the manifest recipe, and these measurements.
Date: 2026-10-10.

Continues `2026-10-05_ensemble.md`, which closed the *composition* question
(YuE2 cannot sing four written parts). This plan is the other half: several
independently rendered takes of the same piece, made to sing **together**.

## 1. Goal

Independent renders drift apart (measured: 50–200 ms at word level, seconds at
section level). This plan makes them one ensemble by aligning every take to one
metric grid, so a listener hears one performance instead of several.

It does **not** create voices, does not change what is sung, and does not make
different singers sound like one person. It fixes *togetherness* only.

Two corrections to earlier statements in this repo's history:

- `rubato` **is** available, so no new dependency class and no Python are needed
  for time-warping or pitch-shifting. The ensemble plan's "no stretcher on this
  box" was wrong.
- Note times exist (`midi::Note.start/end`), so a note-level conductor is
  buildable; the word-level failure below is not the final word.

## 3. Measured evidence (Phase 0)

**Word-level warping fails.** Monotone piecewise-linear warp from word-clock
anchors, leave-one-word-out validated, interior words only (a warp cannot
extrapolate past its own ends), reference = S:

| Part | worst interior residual | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0.19 s | marginal |
| B | 0.98 s | fails |
| T | **2.37 s** | fails |

Section-level offsets reach 4.2 s (T enters the chorus that late). No global or
piecewise stretch on six sparse anchors fixes that: the tenor does not phrase in
even metric values, and stretching cannot smooth arbitrary phrasing.

**Note-level anchors are dense enough to try.** MuScriptor on the *separated
vocal stems*: S 114 notes / 47 distinct onsets, T 70 notes / 53 onsets.

**Two traps, found the hard way:**

1. Transcribing the **full mix** returns chords, not the voice (first onsets
   were 48/55/60/64/67 sounding together). Anchors must come from a vocals stem.
2. Even on a vocals stem, 32–43 % of onsets carry more than one note, and both
   takes were detected **an octave low** (S read as 52/55/57 = E3/G3/A3 for a
   written E4/G4/A4). Consistent within a take, so relative matching survives —
   but pitch matching must be octave-agnostic.

**The good news:** the takes sing the *written* notes in the *written* octaves
(verified by transcription in the ensemble plan). So if timing can be fixed, the
result is a genuinely in-tune ensemble — the harmony is already correct.

## 4. Design: grid-first, not take-to-take

Two takes of **different parts** share rhythm, not pitch, so take-to-take
matching has to run on onset patterns — fragile, and it has no absolute time
reference. The score's own grid is exact and common to all takes, so:

1. **Reference grid** from the score (`M:`, `Q:`, bar lengths). Where no score
   exists, fall back to the beat tracker's beat/downbeat times.
2. **Assign onsets to grid slots.** Per take, from its vocals-stem note list, a
   dynamic program over (onset, duration) pairs, allowing insertions and
   deletions so the model's phrasing differences do not break the alignment.
3. **Fit a low-parameter warp.** Per section: offset + speed by least squares
   over the onsets assigned to that section. Deliberately few parameters: anchor
   jitter sets a noise floor, so per-note snapping cannot beat its own noise.
   Outliers rejected, not averaged in.
4. **Apply** with `rubato::Async`, segment by segment, on the vocals stem.
5. **Report**, always: per-onset residual, unmatched/ambiguous anchors, sections
   skipped, speed ratios used. A take that does not converge is reported, never
   silently "fixed".

## 5. Risks (to be documented in the output, not hidden)

- **Anchor jitter floor.** MuScriptor onset error is tens of ms; no warp can be
  more accurate than the anchors it fits. Expect 20–40 ms, not 5 ms.
- **Octave displacement.** The model may sing a part an octave off (bass up an
  octave has already been observed) → match on pitch class + contour.
- **Repeated identical notes** make correspondence ambiguous → report, never guess.
- **Uneven take lengths.** A take may end early (S/A ended at 21.5 s against a
  28.4 s request) → align only the common span, pad or trim, and state which.
- **Stretch ratio.** Beyond ~1.5× quality degrades; cap, report, and leave the
  section unaligned rather than ruin it.
- **What alignment cannot fix:** timbre, diction, and pronunciation differ
  between takes. The ensemble will be tight but still obviously several singers.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- Onset residual after warping: **median ≤ 20 ms, p95 ≤ 40 ms** (musical
  togetherness is ~30–50 ms; word-level warping measured 190–2370 ms).
- **Pitch unchanged**: re-transcribe the warped take; the pitch sequence must
  match the pre-warp transcription (catches resampling artefacts).
- **Listening test**: two aligned takes plus the bed, no audible flamming across
  the full 8 bars. Concretely: the ensemble plan's failure at **00:00:10** must
  move past the end of the piece.
- Report lists every ambiguous or unmatched anchor.

**Gate:** if the tenor (the worst case, 2.37 s at word level) cannot be brought
under 40 ms on note anchors, drop the idea and say so. Do not ship a partial
conductor.

## 7a. Measured 2026-10-10: what "instrumental" does and does not do

Asked for `instrumental, ... no moving melody` with `[instrumental]` lyrics,
the model sang a tenor verse and a female chorus anyway — **vocalising**,
English-sounding shapes rather than words.

- Both the version without and **with** the Instrumental LoRA
  (`yue2-instrumental`, AR 1.0) came back the same: isolated vocal stem
  rms 0.103 without vs **0.120** with. The LoRA does not measurably suppress
  singing. Hypothesis tested and refuted; recorded so it is not retried.
- The studio's own ASR reports `karaoke.instrumental` for **both**. That check
  therefore proves *no intelligible words*, not *no singing* — which also
  weakens the BED3 verification in `2026-10-05_ensemble.md` to "no words",
  though the score recipe itself holds and the user's ear confirmed it.
- What does work is the **score**: melody on `Ins`, Vocal silent with chord
  symbols only. Style words and the LoRA are advisory; the score is binding.
- Practical rule: when the absence of singing matters, check by ear or by
  vocal-stem energy. Never by ASR alone.

### Conclusion (2026-10-10)

The model **wants to sing**. "Instrumental" — in the style line, in the lyrics,
or as a LoRA — is treated as a suggestion, and what reliably changes is only
whether *words* are sung: without them the model hums, vocalises or brumms.
That is a usable outcome when random and wordless is acceptable. When a track
needs intelligible lyrics, the practical advice is the opposite of what the
style says: **leave "instrumental" out**, and expect text to come out
singable.

### Stems from generated music (same session)

The one genuinely new observation, from stacking a harmonised "other" stem:
separation output from a generated track is not a multitrack. An organ stem
still carries fragments of the vocalised singing, and the artefacts audible in
a stacked stem are those bleed-throughs, not the processing. Source-separation
models are trained mostly on finished mixes rather than isolated stems, so this
is principled and currently unavoidable — and it is *not* specific to the
harmoniser. Recorded in `docs/linux.md` so stem work is planned around it.

## 10. Phase 2 results (2026-10-10) — gate failed, idea dropped

Section-aware assignment (grid bars × LRC chorus-entry windows), per-section
least-squares offset+speed, phantom notes past the stem end clipped. Grid
assumption verified: the rendered S ABC is byte-identical to the regenerated
one. Transcriber instrument labels are unreliable (T/A vocals read as
`acoustic_piano`, B as guitars) — only pitch classes are used, labels ignored.

| Take | Verse median/p95 | Chorus median/p95 | Overall | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 15 pairs, +0.129 s, 1.0017, −6.7/108.9 | **SKIPPED** (needs ~3× speed) | −6.7/108.9 | FAIL |
| A | 15 pairs, +0.109 s, 0.9817, −1.1/66.8 | 5 pairs, +0.198 s, 0.9810, 0.0/14.0 | −0.5/38.8 | PASS (thin: 5 chorus pairs) |
| **T** | 15 pairs, +0.105 s, 0.9789, −5.3/52.1 | 12 pairs, +13.676 s, 0.7794, 6 dropped, −9.0/81.0 | −5.3/**52.1** | **FAIL** |
| B | 12 pairs, +0.287 s, 0.9851, 6 dropped, 50.5/117.9 | 15 pairs, +5.553 s, 1.1656, 11 dropped, 7.9/70.6 | 28.7/117.9 | FAIL |

Gate needs the tenor under 40 ms; it is at 52.1 ms. Per §6 the idea is
dropped, and this is the saying-so:

1. **The medians are excellent and the tails kill it.** Every take's
   systematic timing is grid-true to ~5 ms — then scattered onsets deviate
   50–500 ms from any section line, on correctly matched notes (pitch
   classes agree, so this is not misassignment). A linear warp fixes the
   5 ms it can see and keeps the 100 ms it cannot.
2. **Takes phrase line-by-line with their own prosody.** S sings 4 verse
   lines over 16 s and rushes 4 chorus lines into ~3 s (needs 3× speed —
   refused, correctly); T stretches its chorus to 0.78× starting 13.7 s
   late. No per-section linear warp spans line gaps plus intra-line rush.
   Per-line or per-note warps would "pass" by overfitting 16 parameters to
   30 points, past the anchor jitter floor — which is why the plan forbids
   them, and why this report does not try.
3. **Section windows were load-bearing.** A first global-DP run matched T's
   chorus bars to verse-tail onsets (small residuals, wrong bars): hymn
   sections share pitch material and the time cost saturates. The LRC
   chorus-entry anchors (S 17.25, A 17.06, T 21.00, B 16.22 s) split the
   assignment per section. A conductor without section anchors aligns the
   wrong bars well.
4. **Both transcribers hallucinate past the audio end.** MuScriptor sang
   3.2 s past one 21.5 s stem (S: 60 of 114 notes dropped by stem-length
   clip); Parakeet timed a word at 22.62 s in a 21.456 s song. Phase 0's
   own "S 114 notes / 47 onsets" counted the phantoms. The manifest clips
   to stem length and records the count.
5. **B's vocals stem is contaminated** (224 notes incl. guitars): Demucs
   bleed on a bass take. B was never the gate, but it confirms §7a's stem
   caveat inside the take set itself.

Kept, because they are real and reusable: `align.rs` with its 9 unit tests
plus the ignored gate test (the executable record of this table),
`scripts/choir-align.py` (stems → vocals-stem transcription → anchored
manifest), the section-anchor lesson for any future alignment attempt, and
the knowledge that the takes are grid-true in the large and free in the
small — which is a musical fact about the model, not a bug in the fit.
